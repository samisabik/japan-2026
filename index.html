Second-hand clothes

Shimokitazawa is the best area for this. Harajuku and Koenji are good too.

  • 10towMust go. Small 4th-floor shop of Japanese designer vintage: Comme des Garçons, Yohji, Issey. Curated, not cheap.
  • KindalSecond-hand designer chain: Loewe, Prada, Hysteric Glamour. Good prices on premium brands, less so on basics. Six branches: Shimokitazawa, Shibuya, Harajuku, Shinjuku, Nakameguro, Daikanyama.
  • Sui Vintage"Vintage for adults": well-kept men's and women's pieces, mostly ¥10,000–20,000. Opens 2pm.
  • Furugiya MementoNewer select shop mixing US and European vintage, leaning design and mode pieces.
  • NOTIME60s–70s American counterculture, psychedelic prints and cheap band tees. Open late, partly self-service.
  • TreFac StyleSecond-hand chain with fair prices, mostly ¥4,000–9,000, and good designer and outdoor brands. Two shops in Shimokitazawa.
  • Atlantis VintageAuthentic vintage luxury bags and jewellery, Chanel and Louis Vuitton. Friendly owner, tax-free. Koenji.
  • SalamancaDesigner vintage in a Harajuku flat. Known for well-priced Issey Miyake. Tax-free.
  • Brand CollectDesigner and luxury second-hand, Hermès to streetwear, in like-new condition. English-speaking staff, tax-free.
  • KomehyoJapan's biggest second-hand department store, 8 floors in Shinjuku: bags, watches, Comme des Garçons to Supreme.
  • EcostyleBrand resale shop: designer clothes, leather jackets and shoes. 7F, near Shinjuku-sanchome.
  • OkuraDaikanyama shop built like an old storehouse, selling clothes hand-dyed in natural indigo. All made in Japan.

Books, art and junk

  • Book Off, Hard Off, 2nd StreetChain stores. Hard Off is the one for electronic junk: untested gear, old synths and cameras at "junk" prices.
  • AkihabaraElectronics town. Beyond the anime, old-school parts shops under the tracks, second-hand instruments and books. Old synth bits.
  • NyankodoTiny shop inside Anekawa Bookstore with 2,000+ books about cats, and cute original tote bags. In the old book district.
  • Hikarie bookstore, 8th floorHikarie's creative floor, with the d47 design museum and shop: crafts and design from all 47 prefectures.
  • Pigment TokyoArt supply shop that feels like a gallery: 4,000+ pigments in jars under a Kengo Kuma bamboo ceiling. Closed Mondays.
  • 2k540 Aki-Oka ArtisanAbout 50 makers' workshops under the tracks: leather, ceramics, wood, a kaleidoscope shop. Closed Wednesdays.
  • Neko Machi GalleryCat art gallery in an old Yanaka house: detailed cat dolls downstairs, cheaper cat pieces upstairs. Thu–Sun only.
  • Nakano Broadway1966 arcade full of vintage toys and retro games. Mandarake Henya for antique toys. Next to Fujiya Camera.

Music and synths

  • Five G"The best modular shop I've been to." Legendary vintage synth and Eurorack shop; you can try gear. Pricey. 4F, Harajuku.
  • Happy End BeansModular shop and coffee. Colourful coffee stand full of retro knick-knacks, pour-over from its own beans.
  • Ishibashi ShibuyaBig name in used instruments since 1938. Some nice vintage synths and drum machines, at full prices. BEAM building 2F.
  • Echigoya ShibuyaTiny 9th-floor shop of used synths and drum machines, from Roland tape echoes to 808s. Pricey but trustworthy. Opposite Tower Records.
  • Meidai-dori, OchanomizuA ten-minute street of guitar and music shops, new and used. Easy to compare prices. Some sell synths.
  • Uptown Record StoreKoenji shop for experimental, synth, post-punk and Chinese underground records. Listening decks and beer.

Cameras

  • Fujiya CameraFair-priced film cameras. The Junk Hall next door has bargain bins of point-and-shoots, sold as-is. Nakano.
  • Five Star CameraPoint-and-shoots and film gear, prices on every item. English-speaking staff. Hamayu Bldg 2F, Nishi-Shinjuku.
  • Used Camera BoxTiny basement shop packed with film compacts. The owner tests before you buy. Cash only. Next door to Five Star.

Outdoor gear

  • Ishii Sports Tozan HontenFlagship mountain shop, 100+ brands including Rab and Black Diamond. Takii Tokyo Bldg 2F, Jimbocho.
  • Sakaiya SportsJimbocho mountain-gear institution with expert staff. Stocks Rab. Several buildings on the same street.
  • Ishii Sports Shinjuku NishiguchiUnder Yodobashi, B1 and B2. Stocks Rab. Two minutes from the camera shops.
  • maunga KichijojiSecond-hand outdoor gear, cleaned and repaired. Best bet for a cheap rain jacket. Nakamichi-dori.

Garden tools

  • MoriheiOld blade and whetstone dealer since 1933, crammed with hand-forged pruning shears and garden knives made for professional gardeners. You can try sharpening stones. Weekdays only, 9:00–17:30. Asakusabashi.
  • UbukeyaTiny blade shop since 1783, so sharp "it cuts a baby's hair". Ikebana flower shears, scissors and tweezers rather than big pruners. Closed Sundays. Ningyocho.
  • Vintage garden toolsNo dedicated shop found. Look for old Japanese shears and trowels at the Oedo Antique Market and Hanazono Shrine flea market (To see tab), and in Hard Off junk corners.

Museums

  • Extinct Media MuseumAbout 1,500 old gadgets, from early film cameras to Walkmans and a NeXT cube, and you can pick them up and play. ¥2,000.
  • Museum of Roadside ArtEditor Kyoichi Tsuzuki's three floors of Showa kitsch, sideshow relics and erotic museum leftovers, with a bar and karaoke. Adult content. Mukojima.
  • WHAT MuseumWarehouse turned museum of contemporary art and architecture models, shelves of them you walk between. Next to Pigment.
  • Meguro Parasitological MuseumThe world's only parasite museum, home to an 8.8 m tapeworm, with a ribbon to show you how long that is. Small, weird and free.
  • teamLab PlanetsImmersive digital art you walk through barefoot, including knee-deep water and a room of floating orbs. Book ahead.
  • Nezu MuseumAsian art in a Kengo Kuma building, with a deep, hilly garden of teahouses and stone lanterns behind it.
  • Museum of Contemporary Art (MOT)Tokyo's big contemporary art museum, 5,500+ works in a striking building. Kiyosumi-Shirakawa, full of cafes and galleries.
  • Tokyo National MuseumJapan's oldest and biggest museum: samurai armour, swords, scrolls and Buddhist sculpture. Ueno Park.
  • Odaiba Takoyaki MuseumFood court of five famous Osaka takoyaki makers. Watch them flip the balls. Decks mall, 4F.
  • IntermediathequeFree University of Tokyo museum of skeletons and old instruments in antique cabinets. KITTE 2–3F. Closed Mondays.

Sights

  • Senso-jiTokyo's oldest temple, reached through the giant red Kaminarimon lantern and a street of snack stalls. Go early or in the evening.
  • GotokujiBirthplace of the lucky cat: thousands of white maneki-neko. Free. Take the cat-themed Setagaya tram there.
  • Yoyogi ParkBig, relaxed park next to Meiji Shrine's forest. Weekend buskers, dancers and picnics.
  • KabukizaTokyo's grand kabuki theatre. Buy a single-act ticket on the day for a short, cheap first taste. Ginza.
  • Okuno Building1932 luxury apartments now full of tiny galleries and antique shops, with an old manual lift. Room 306, untouched since its last tenant, opens on the 6th of the month.
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government BuildingFree observation decks 202 m up, with views to Fuji on clear days. Shinjuku.

Neighbourhoods to wander

  • ShimokitazawaYour favourite. Low-rise streets of second-hand shops, small theatres, live houses and bars. Relaxed.
  • YanesenYanaka, Nezu and Sendagi: old wooden Tokyo that survived the war, with temples, a cat-filled cemetery and Yanaka Ginza shopping street. Info centre.
  • NakameguroCanal lined with trees, small boutiques, coffee and design shops. Calm and pretty.
  • Kuramae"Tokyo's Brooklyn": old warehouse district turned craft quarter. Coffee roasters, stationery, leather and a bean-to-bar chocolate maker.
  • NingyochoOld doll-makers' quarter that survived the quake and the war. Amazake Yokocho: 400 m of family shops, tofu, rice crackers and warm sweet amazake.
  • ShinbashiSalaryman drinking district. Tiny smoky izakaya and yakitori counters under the train tracks, cheap and loud since the 1950s.
  • OdaibaMan-made island in Tokyo Bay: Rainbow Bridge views, the life-size Gundam, and teamLab nearby.
  • IkebukuroBusy hub with Otome Road anime shops, Sunshine City and huge department stores.
  • Todoroki ValleyA 1 km wooded ravine walk along a stream, noticeably cooler than the city, ending at a temple and a tea house. Reopened 2026.
  • ChofuEerie and strange. Manga artist Shigeru Mizuki's home town: yokai statues on Kitaro street, and Jindaiji, the "yokai temple".
  • Yamanote lineRide the loop and get off somewhere random.
  • Koenji100+ vintage shops near the south exit, plus records and live houses. Like Shimokitazawa, rougher and cheaper.

Kyoto

  • Nintendo MuseumNintendo's old Uji factory, from 1889 hanafuda cards to the Switch, with playable classics. Tickets by lottery; spares sometimes go on sale. Closed Tuesdays.
  • MinamizaKabuki in Gion, at Japan's oldest theatre site, 400+ years on the same spot. Current building from 1929.
  • EnryakujiWorld Heritage temple complex spread through forest on Mount Hiei. Dim, smoky main hall, trails between temples, Lake Biwa views. Half a day.

Markets and galleries

  • Oedo Antique MarketJapan's biggest outdoor antique market, 250+ stalls outside Tokyo International Forum, usually 1st and 3rd Sundays.
  • Hanazono Shrine flea marketSundays, early until about 15:00. Not on if it rains. Bring cash, and a stiff folder for prints.
  • Ryuichi Sakamoto: async – immersionUntil 25 Oct. From ¥2,500. Tickets

Day trips

  • HakoneHot springs, the Open-Air Museum's sculpture park, the steaming Owakudani valley and Fuji views. About 1.5 hours.
  • KamakuraThe quiet side: a medieval quarry wall, 160+ samurai burial caves at Mandarado and mossy old passes. Lunch at Juoh Rock.
  • NikkoOrnate shrines in a cedar forest, then up to Yumoto onsen town: a 2-hour walk around Lake Yuno and a free foot bath.
  • ChichibuPilgrim country: Enyuji's Iwaido hall, reached by 300 mossy steps and built into a cliff on stilts, and the 156 m Urayama Dam.
  • Mount NokogiriOld quarry mountain with Japan's largest pre-modern stone Buddha (31 m) and the "Hell Lookout" cliff edge. Hike up from Hama-Kanaya, ropeway down.
  • Chiba coastOkitsu to Onjuku: beaches, sea caves, and an observatory tower where you go 8 m under the sea to watch fish. Take the Wakashio express.
  • SaitamaOmiya, 30 minutes out: a 5th-century shrine, a village of bonsai gardens and the huge Railway Museum, all walkable in a day.
  • Underground flood tunnelHuge "dystopian" concrete halls. The "underground temple": a flood tank of 59 giant pillars. Tours from ¥1,000, book weeks ahead.
  • KaruizawaMountain resort 70 minutes by shinkansen. Forest walks, Kumoba Pond, Shiraito Falls, and early autumn colour.
  • Ikaho OnsenHot spring town on a volcano's slope: 365 stone steps lined with shops and free foot baths, and iron-rich "golden" water.
  • UtsunomiyaGyoza capital, an hour by shinkansen. Pair it with the Oya History Museum, a vast cold underground stone quarry lit like a cathedral.

Sushi, ramen and yakitori

LLM picks from web research, not friends' recommendations. Walk-in or queue, good value, away from tourist areas.

  • Sushi no Midori, UmegaokaSushi. The original branch of a local favourite, famous for generous, cheap sets (about ¥2,000–4,000). Ticket queue at the door; reservations also taken. A minute from Umegaoka station, near Gotokuji.
  • Tachigui Sushi Akira, ShinbashiSushi. 7-person standing counter from a top omakase chef; reviewers call it omakase quality at a fraction of the price. Lunch ¥6–8k, dinner more. First come, first served, hourly seatings. Closed Wednesdays.
  • Harukiya, OgikuboRamen. Old-school Tokyo shoyu since 1949, light sardine-scented broth. 18 seats, walk in, card only. Ogikubo is two stops past Koenji.
  • Kamunabi, SendagiRamen. Family-run shoyu ramen with a famously huge slab of chashu; on Tabelog's top-100 list every year. 13 seats, no reservations, cash only, closes when sold out. Closed Mon–Tue. Near Yanesen.
  • Iseya Sohonten, KichijojiYakitori. Smoky, cheap skewers on the charcoal since 1928, locals and a queue at the grill window. Cash only, closed Tuesdays. By Inokashira Park.
  • Uchida, TateishiYakiton (pork offal skewers). 70-year-old Showa bar in Tokyo's most old-fashioned drinking town, around ¥200 a plate. Strict house rules: Japanese-only ordering, no phones on the counter. Brave but real.

Food in Kyoto

  • Moon and BackRamen bar and brunch cafe by Nishiki Market. Known for wagyu ramen torched at the table and proper gluten-free ramen.

Bars and live music

  • TomboyShimokitazawa. Your favourite bar; the owner is lovely.
  • Beat CafeWord-of-mouth basement dive bar run by an indie promoter. Cheap big beers; the toilet walls are signed by Hot Chip, Soulwax and more.
  • Golden GaiAbout 200 tiny bars packed into six alleys, each seating a handful. Every one has its own theme. Shinjuku.
  • Nonbei Yokocho"Drunkard's Alley": two lanes of about 40 tiny 1950s bars by Shibuya Crossing, many with only 4–8 seats. Expect a small cover charge.
  • Harmonica YokochoPost-war black-market lanes turned into tiny bars and yakitori counters under red lanterns. Some locals drink from noon. Kichijoji.
  • Ochiai SoupUnderground venue for experimental and independent music, in a basement under a public bath. Near Higashi-Nakano.
  • JBS (Jazz, Blues, Soul)Tiny listening bar, 11,000 records, full LPs. Drinks ¥500. Keep it quiet, no requests. Dogenzaka.
